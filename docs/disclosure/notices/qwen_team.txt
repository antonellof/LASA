Subject: Heads-up: refusal-direction transfer measured on Qwen/Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B-Instruct, Qwen/Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B-Instruct

Hello Qwen team (Alibaba),

I am Antonello Fratepietro, a security researcher. Ahead of a preprint on inherited jailbreak
risk in fine-tuned open-weight models, I am sharing a finding about a checkpoint
you publish, Qwen/Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B-Instruct, Qwen/Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B-Instruct, as a courtesy before publication.

Using the public refusal-direction method of Arditi et al. (2024), I extracted the
refusal direction from Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct and applied it as an activation ablation to
Qwen/Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B-Instruct, Qwen/Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B-Instruct. Across 12 JailbreakBench behaviors, 5 generations each, measured
against a no-attack control, this raised the non-refusal rate as follows: Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B-Instruct +10.0 pts (RefCos +0.3627); Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B-Instruct +13.3 pts (RefCos +0.3824). An automated label
was used, so the figure is an upper bound on actual harmful compliance; see the
limitations section of the paper.

This is not a new attack. It confirms, on your checkpoint, the known result that a
base model's refusal direction transfers to its derivatives. I am not sharing any
harmful generations. The method, the aggregate numbers, and the code are in the
paper and repository.
